    OPERA research group · IGE Grenoble

    Open computational earth and climate science

    We study the atmosphere, the ocean and the cryosphere in the context of climate change — developing the models, methods and open tools that tomorrow's climate science needs.

    OPERA lives at the meeting point of two traditions of our discipline: the community models that have anchored climate science for decades, and the emerging generation of data-driven and hybrid methods. Rather than choosing between them, we practice both — and work where they learn from each other.

    Research themes

    Three components of the Earth system, studied in the context of climate change — from process understanding to models, data and predictions.

    Atmosphere

    Aerosols, clouds and atmospheric chemistry; atmosphere–surface interactions.

    Ocean

    Near-surface ocean processes, air–sea exchanges, modelling and remote sensing.

    Cryosphere

    Sea ice, snow, glaciers — and their coupling with the ocean and atmosphere.

    Advancing computational methods

    We develop, calibrate and evaluate process-based community models; we explore data-driven and hybrid approaches; and we rethink the design of the numerical systems that carry our science.

    Improving process-based models

    Development, calibration, evaluation and uncertainty quantification of community models.

    Exploring data-driven approaches

    Hybrid modelling, differentiable programming, neural data assimilation.

    Enhancing numerical system design

    Modularity, interoperability, and revisiting the APIs of geoscientific models.
